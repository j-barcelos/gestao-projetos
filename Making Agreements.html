Lesson outcome

By the end, you can close a simple deal

  • Ask clear questions about an IT service.
  • Answer a client's questions politely.
  • Say yes and say no without losing the deal.
  • Confirm details and agree a price.

Speaking · Language bank A

Asking about the company's service

How many technicians / engineers are there?
How about coming before / after 8 o'clock?
Can you / your team do that?
How much time do you need?
Can you provide the software licences?
How much is that for fifteen hours a week?
Does the price include the licences?
When can your team start?

Swap the words in bold: engineers, devices, cables, maintenance, cybersecurity.

07 / 13
TeacherModel, then drill the intonation of "How about coming before eight?" — it is a suggestion, not a question word order. Keep the substitution drill fast and mechanical.
Speaking · Language bank B

Asking the client questions

What do you want the technician to do, exactly?
How many days / hours do you need the service?
What time do you want the team to come?
Is there a place for the equipment / server?
How about on Monday?
Is three hours a day enough for you?

Notice the pattern: What do you want + person + to do? · How many + hours + do you need?

08 / 13
TeacherPoint out that these questions put the number or time in a fixed slot. Then have students build three new questions for the laptop setup situation before moving on.
Responding

Saying yes and saying no

Saying yes

  • Yes, we can.
  • Yes, it does.
  • Yes, that's fine.
  • Yes, that's right.
  • Yes, of course.

Short, clear, then move to the next detail.

Saying no

  • No, we can't. I'm sorry.
  • No, it doesn't. I'm sorry.
  • No, we don't — we have a specialist company.
  • No, but we can offer something else.

Always add a reason or an alternative. Never just "No".

09 / 13
TeacherInsist on the apology and the alternative. Drill the pair as a chain: A asks, B refuses with a reason, A accepts the alternative. Two minutes, then swap.
Strategy

From "no" to "not yet"

A refusal is not the end of the meeting. Keep the deal alive.

01

Refuse the item, not the client

"No, we can't repair the printers — but we can check the network."

02

Ask a question instead

"What if we try two hours a day instead of three?"

03

Give the reason

"That might be difficult — the licences cost extra."

04

Confirm what you both agree

"So, the setup and the backups — yes. The printers — no."

10 / 13
TeacherBoard the four moves. Then upgrade: take one flat "No" from the class and rebuild it together into a counter-offer.
Roleplay 1 · IT support contract

Negotiate the contract

Student A · Robert's IT Services
  • Ask how many computers the office has.
  • Offer two technicians, three hours a day.
  • Quote £250 a week. Software licences are a small extra charge.
  • Say no to repairing the printers — specialist company.
  • Agree a start date. Confirm the details in writing.
    • You want the contract. Be flexible on hours, not on price.
    Student B · Office manager
  • Describe the office: reception, two offices, a server room, two printers.
  • List the IT tasks you need.
  • Ask how many technicians and how much time they need.
  • Ask if the price includes the licences. Ask for a lower price.
  • Ask when they can start.
    • Your budget is tight. You still need the service.

    Three minutes. Then swap roles.

    Roleplay 2 · New laptops for the team

    On-site or remote setup?

    Student A · You need the setup
  • Ask if they set up laptops on-site or remotely.
  • Say how many laptops it is for.
  • Say the day and time of the setup.
  • Ask how many technicians they send.
  • Ask if the team can transfer the old files too.
  • Ask if they provide chargers and adapters.
  • Ask how much they charge.
    • It's a Friday morning setup. About 25 laptops.
    Student B · IT services company
  • Answer: on-site or remote setup.
  • Ask how many laptops it's for.
  • Ask the day and time.
  • Say how many technicians you send for each option.
  • Ask if there is good Wi-Fi at the office.
  • Answer about chargers and adapters.
  • Give your price per laptop.
    • On-site setup: 3 technicians. Remote setup: 1 technician. Chargers and adapters included.
